77 | Executive Summary 
78 | The RCC-APS or Republic Central Colleges Automated Payroll System is a web-based payroll management system developed for Republic Central Colleges. It is designed to automate the computation of employee salaries, mandatory government deductions, and net pay, providing a modern, automated, and legally compliant payroll solution for the institution.The system is built for three types of users. The Finance Officer uses the system to process the monthly payroll, generate payslips, and produce financial reports. The Regular Employee uses the Employee Dashboard to view and download their digital payslips and update their personal contact information. The System Administrator manages all user accounts and role assignments within the system.The RCC-APS is developed using the following technologies: Vanilla JavaScript for the frontend user interface, Node.js with Express.js for the backend server and API, MySQL for the database, and JWT (JSON Web Tokens) for secure authentication and role-based access control. The system is developed following the Agile software development methodology.The system automatically computes the Gross Pay, SSS contribution, PhilHealth premium, Pag-IBIG contribution, BIR withholding tax under the TRAIN Law, and the final Net Pay of each employee, all in compliance with Philippine labor and tax regulations.

83 | Background of the Study
84 | 
85 | Payroll management is one of the most fundamental administrative functions in any organization. It involves the systematic computation of employee wages, application of mandatory government deductions, and timely delivery of accurate compensation. According to the Society for Human Resource Management (SHRM, 2023), organizations that implement automated payroll solutions experience a significant reduction in processing time and human error rates compared to less automated approaches. Effective payroll systems not only ensure accurate compensation but also reinforce employee trust and institutional compliance.
86 | 
87 | In the Philippine context, payroll management carries additional complexity due to government-mandated contribution programs that every employer must comply with. These include the Social Security System (SSS) under Republic Act No. 11199 (Republic of the Philippines, 2018), the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth) under Republic Act No. 11223 (Republic of the Philippines, 2019), the Home Development Mutual Fund or Pag-IBIG under Republic Act No. 9679 (Republic of the Philippines, 2009), and the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) withholding tax under the TRAIN Law or Republic Act No. 10963 (Department of Finance, 2017). Each of these agencies prescribes specific contribution rates and computation rules that must be accurately applied in every payroll cycle. Non-compliance results in penalties, back payments, and reputational damage to the institution.
88 | 
89 | Several studies have demonstrated the benefits of automating payroll and administrative processes in institutional settings. Agrawal and Bansal (2013) noted that even automated systems, if not properly designed, can suffer from poor data accuracy and limited analytical capabilities, highlighting the importance of well-engineered payroll solutions. Research by Verma and Gupta (2013) further emphasized that automation reduces administrative workload and minimizes the risk of errors in government contribution computations — a critical concern for Philippine institutions.
90 | 
91 | Role-based access control (RBAC), as described by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST, 2006), is recognized as a security best practice for enterprise information systems. By restricting access to system features based on user roles, RBAC ensures data integrity, reduces unauthorized access risk, and promotes accountability. Commercial payroll platforms available in the Philippines, such as Sprout Solutions and GreatDay HR (SHRM, 2023), demonstrate that this principle is widely applied in modern payroll systems. However, these commercial solutions are primarily designed for large enterprises and may not fully address the specific operational context of an educational institution such as Republic Central Colleges.
92 | 
93 | A review of related capstone and academic projects on payroll automation in Philippine institutions reveals common limitations — most student-developed systems lack full compliance with the BIR TRAIN Law tax schedule, do not integrate with a separate Human Resource Information System (HRIS), or are limited to a single user type without differentiated access controls. These gaps indicate a clear opportunity to develop a more comprehensive, legally compliant, and institutionally tailored payroll solution.
94 | 
95 | Guided by these findings and the established importance of accurate payroll management, this capstone project developed the RCC-APS: Republic Central Colleges Automated Payroll System — a web-based payroll management system designed to provide Republic Central Colleges with a modern, automated, and legally compliant payroll solution. The RCC-APS was built to deliver the following outputs for the institution: automated payroll computation covering Gross Pay, all mandatory government deductions, and Net Pay; digital payslip generation and PDF download for employees; payroll summary and contribution reports for the Finance Officer; role-based access control for three user classes; and a deduction table management module for maintaining updated government contribution rates.
96 | 
97 | By developing the RCC-APS, this project seeks to contribute a purpose-built, institution-specific solution that addresses the payroll management needs of Republic Central Colleges, while serving as a practical demonstration of the knowledge and skills developed by the team throughout their academic program.

102 | Objectives of the Study
103 | 
104 |   The general objective of this study is to design, develop, and evaluate a web-based automated payroll system for Republic Central Colleges that accurately computes employee salaries, mandatory government deductions, and net pay in compliance with Philippine labor and tax laws.
105 | 
106 | Specifically, the study aims to:
107 | 
108 | automate the computation of employee Gross Pay, government deductions (SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, and BIR withholding tax), and Net Pay in compliance with the Social Security Act of 2018, the Universal Health Care Act, the HDMF Law, and the TRAIN Law;
109 | develop a secure, role-based web application that provides distinct dashboard access for three user classes: Finance Officer, Regular Employee, and System Administrator;
110 | implement a digital payslip generation and PDF download feature accessible through the Employee Dashboard, enabling employees to view and retain their compensation records;
111 | build a Reporting Module that allows the Finance Officer to generate payroll summary reports and government contribution reports and export them in PDF format;
112 | create a Deduction Table Management Module that allows the Finance Officer to update government contribution rates without requiring changes to the system's source code;
113 | integrate the system with the partner HRIS to receive employee records and deduction rate inputs, eliminating the need for duplicate data entry; and
114 | evaluate the system's accuracy, usability, and functionality through user acceptance testing conducted with simulated end users for each user class.

157 | Scope and Delimitations
158 | 
159 | This section describes the scope of the RCC-APS and clarifies its intentional exclusions. It assists stakeholders in forming accurate expectations about the system's purpose, coverage, and limitations.
160 | 
161 | Scope
162 | 
163 | The RCC-APS covers the following functionalities:
164 | 
165 | User authentication and role-based access control for three distinct user classes: Finance Officer, Regular Employee, and System Administrator, each with a dedicated dashboard and feature set;
166 | Payroll computation including Gross Pay calculation, automatic deduction of SSS contributions (based on the official salary bracket table), PhilHealth premium (5% of basic monthly salary, 2.5% employee share), Pag-IBIG contributions (2% of monthly compensation, capped at ₱100.00 per month), BIR withholding tax (computed using the TRAIN Law annual tax schedule), and final Net Pay;
167 | Digital payslip generation with a detailed breakdown of earnings and deductions, and employee-accessible PDF download;
168 | Government contribution and payroll summary report generation with export functionality in PDF format;
169 | User account management including creation, removing, deactivation, and reactivation of system accounts by the System Administrator;
170 | Deduction table management allowing the Finance Officer to update SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, and BIR contribution rates; and
171 | Integration with the partner HRIS system for receiving employee data and deduction rate inputs.
172 | 
173 | Delimitations
174 | 
175 | The following are outside the scope of the RCC-APS:
176 | 
177 | The system does not process actual bank transfers, e-wallet disbursements, or any form of salary payment transaction;
178 | Employee attendance tracking, biometric integration, and time-keeping are not handled by the RCC-APS. These functions remain within the scope of the partner HRIS system;
179 | Overtime filing, approval, and overtime pay computation are not included in the system's scope;
180 | Employee hiring, recruitment, onboarding, and scheduling are managed by the HRIS and are entirely outside the scope of the RCC-APS; and
181 | The system does not replicate or replace the full functionality of the HRIS. It only receives input data from the HRIS and does not write back to it.

183 | Essential Definitions
184 | 
185 | For the purposes of this study, the following terms are defined as used in the context of the RCC-APS:
186 | 
187 | Agile. a flexible, iterative approach to software development where work is divided into sprints, enabling continuous feedback, rapid adjustments, and incremental delivery of functional system components. In the RCC-APS, Agile methodology guided the project through planning, design, development, testing, and deployment phases across six sprints.
188 | 
189 | Automated Payroll System. a web-based software application that automatically computes employee wages, mandatory government contributions, and net pay based on pre-programmed rules and government-mandated deduction tables, providing automated, rules-based payroll computation.
190 | 
191 | BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue). the Philippine government agency responsible for the assessment and collection of national taxes. In the RCC-APS, BIR refers to the monthly withholding tax deducted from employee salaries, computed using the annual tax schedule prescribed under the TRAIN Law (Republic Act No. 10963).
192 | 
193 | Deduction. any amount subtracted from an employee's Gross Pay. In the RCC-APS, deductions refer specifically to mandatory government contributions: SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, and BIR withholding tax.
194 | 
195 | Finance Officer. a user class in the RCC-APS assigned to process the monthly payroll, generate digital payslips, produce financial reports, and manage deduction rate tables. The Finance Officer does not have access to user account management features.
196 | 
197 | Gross Pay. the total monthly compensation of an employee before any deductions. In the RCC-APS, Gross Pay is defined as the employee's Basic Monthly Salary as received from the partner HRIS system.
198 | 
199 | HRIS (Human Resource Information System). a software system developed by a partner team (Group 1) that maintains official employee records and provides the RCC-APS with employee data and deduction rate inputs. The HRIS is the authoritative source of employee information for the system.
200 | 
201 | Net Pay. the final take-home salary of an employee after all mandatory deductions (SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, and BIR withholding tax) have been subtracted from the Gross Pay.
202 | 
203 | Pag-IBIG (Home Development Mutual Fund). a government-mandated savings and housing loan program administered by the HDMF. Employee contribution is computed at 2% of monthly compensation, with a maximum contribution of ₱100.00 per month, as per Republic Act No. 9679.
204 | 
205 | Payslip. an official digital document generated by the RCC-APS that provides an employee with a detailed breakdown of their monthly Gross Pay, individual deductions, and final Net Pay for a specific payroll period. Employees may view and download payslips in PDF format through the Employee Dashboard.
206 | 
207 | PhilHealth. the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation, which administers the national health insurance program. The premium rate is 5% of the basic monthly salary, shared equally at 2.5% between the employee and employer, in compliance with Republic Act No. 11223.
208 | 
209 | RCC-APS. the abbreviation for Republic Central Colleges Automated Payroll System, the subject of this capstone project.
210 | 
211 | Regular Employee. a user class in the RCC-APS representing general staff members of the organization. Employees may log in to view and download their payslips, and update personal contact information. They do not have access to payroll processing, reporting, or user management features.
212 | 
213 | Role-Based Access Control (RBAC). a security and access management model implemented in the RCC-APS that restricts each user to only the features and data appropriate to their assigned role. The three defined roles are Finance Officer, Regular Employee, and System Administrator.
214 | 
215 | SSS (Social Security System). a government-mandated social insurance program for private sector employees in the Philippines. Employee contributions are computed using the official SSS monthly salary credit and contribution table, as prescribed by Republic Act No. 11199.
216 | 
217 | System Administrator. a user class in the RCC-APS responsible exclusively for managing user accounts. The System Administrator can create new accounts, assign roles, deactivate accounts, and reactivate suspended accounts. The System Administrator has no access to payroll data or financial reports.
218 | 
219 | TRAIN Law (Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion Act). Republic Act No. 10963, which restructured the Philippine personal income tax schedule. Individuals with annual taxable income of up to ₱250,000 are fully exempt. Progressive tax rates apply to income brackets above this threshold.

275 | During the initial phase, the Development Team identified the Product Owners of the RCC-APS — representing the Finance Officer, Regular Employee, and System Administrator (ITS) roles — who would represent the needs of the institution and guide the development process. The team based the system's requirements on these identified roles, common payroll processes observed within the institution, and applicable Philippine labor law standards. Research was also conducted on existing payroll systems used in Philippine institutions, including commercial solutions such as Sprout Solutions and GreatDay HR, to identify useful features, common limitations, and opportunities for improvement.

This process ensured that both user expectations and Philippine government compliance standards were reflected in the system design. All findings were documented as user stories and prioritized in the product backlog, which became the foundation for sprint planning throughout the development of the RCC-APS.

297 | Design
298 | 
299 | During the design phase, the team started by outlining the system modules, dividing the RCC-APS into key functional areas: Login Module (M1), User Management Module (M2), Payroll Calculation Module (M3), Employee Dashboard Module (M4), Reporting Module (M5), and Deduction Table Management Module (M6). 323 | Development
324 | 
325 | Development proceeded in iterative sprints, each focused on coding and testing specific system features. The team began by implementing the Login Module (M1) with JSON Web Token (JWT) authentication to ensure secure access for all user types and to enforce role-based redirection to the appropriate dashboard.
326 | 
327 | In the subsequent sprints, the Payroll Calculation Engine was developed as the core of the system. This included the implementation of the official SSS contribution bracket lookup, PhilHealth premium computation (5% rate, 2.5% employee share), Pag-IBIG contribution (2% capped at ₱100.00), and BIR withholding tax calculation under the TRAIN Law, which involved annualizing the monthly salary, applying the progressive tax bracket, and deriving the monthly withholding tax. The Net Pay computation was then integrated to produce the final take-home pay of each employee.
328 | 
329 | The team then built the User Management Module (M2) for the System Administrator, which provided full Create, Read, Update, and Delete (CRUD) functionality for user accounts — including the ability to deactivate and reactivate accounts as needed. The Employee Dashboard Module (M4) followed, providing employees with self-service access to view their payslips, download payslips as PDF files, and update their personal contact information.
330 | 
331 | The Reporting Module (M5) was then developed for the Finance Officer, allowing the generation and export of payroll summary reports and government contribution reports in PDF format. Finally, the Deduction Table Management Module (M6) was implemented, enabling the Finance Officer to update SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, and BIR rates without modifying the system source code.
332 | 
333 | Throughout development, the frontend was built using Vanilla JavaScript with HTML5, CSS3, and custom CSS. The backend was developed in Node.js with Express.js, and the database was managed using MySQL. Full backend and frontend integration was completed using RESTful APIs, with JWT-based authentication enforced on every endpoint.
334 | 
335 | Git and GitHub supported version control and collaboration throughout the development. API testing and unit testing of payroll computation functions were conducted. Trello was used for sprint task management and team coordination. Final refinements included UI polishing, bug fixing, and performance tuning, confirming the system's readiness for testing and deployment.

342 | Testing
343 | 
344 | Before the official deployment, several tests were conducted to ensure that the RCC-APS was stable and functioning properly. For unit testing, each module was tested individually, including the Login Module, User Management, Payroll Calculation, Employee Dashboard, Reporting, and Deduction Table Management. Particular emphasis was placed on testing the Payroll Calculation Engine, where each formula — SSS bracket lookup, PhilHealth rate application, Pag-IBIG cap enforcement, BIR TRAIN Law tax annualization, and Net Pay derivation — was validated against manually computed expected results using the official government contribution tables.
345 | 
346 | Integration testing followed to ensure that the combined modules and their linkages worked smoothly and without errors. This included verifying the data flow from the HRIS sync module to the Calculation Engine, from the Calculation Engine to Payslip Generation, and from the Reporting Module to the export functionality. Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) was also tested across all three user dashboards to ensure that each user could only access features appropriate to their role. All backend API endpoints were tested to confirm correct request and response behavior.
347 | 
348 | 
349 | 
350 | After confirming that all modules and integrations were stable, the entire system underwent system testing to assess its overall performance and ensure all features functioned correctly in a complete operational environment. User Acceptance Testing (UAT) was then performed, where simulated end users representing each user class — Finance Officer, Regular Employee, and System Administrator — performed defined test scenarios on the completed system. Feedback was gathered to assess usability, accuracy, and overall user satisfaction. Results were recorded and evaluated against the defined acceptance criteria to confirm that all functional requirements were met.
351 | 
352 | Performance testing involved observing system response time and stability during payroll processing and report generation, while security testing focused on protecting payroll data, employee records, and authentication tokens from unauthorized access. Regression testing was conducted after every bug fix to ensure that no new issues were introduced in previously working features, particularly in the Calculation Engine which underlies both payroll processing and payslip generation. These tests confirmed that the RCC-APS was ready for deployment and capable of supporting the payroll operations of Republic Central Colleges.

385 | RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
386 | This section presents and interprets the key outcomes of the RCC-APS based on the methodology described in the preceding chapter. It discusses how the completed system meets each of the objectives of the study, presents the sample outputs produced by the system, and highlights the insights and issues encountered during the development process. Detailed outputs, screenshots, and other supporting materials may be found in the Project Artifacts section.
387 | System Development Results
388 | All six modules of the RCC-APS — the Login Module (M1), User Management Module (M2), Payroll Calculation Module (M3), Employee Dashboard Module (M4), Reporting Module (M5), and Deduction Table Management Module (M6) — were successfully designed, developed, integrated, and tested within the six Agile sprints described in the methodology. The completed application runs as a single-page web application built with Vanilla JavaScript (HTML5, CSS3, and custom CSS), supported by a Node.js and Express.js backend connected to a MySQL database, with JWT-based authentication and role-based access control for the three user classes. Screenshots of the implemented modules are presented in the Project Artifacts section.
389 | Payroll Computation Results
390 | The Payroll Calculation Engine (M3) was validated against the official SSS salary credit table (Republic Act No. 11199), the 5% PhilHealth premium rate with a 2.5% employee share (Republic Act No. 11223), the Pag-IBIG contribution computed at 2% of monthly compensation and capped at ₱100.00 (Republic Act No. 9679), and the BIR withholding tax schedule under the TRAIN Law (Republic Act No. 10963). Each computation formula produced results that matched the manual computations performed using the official contribution tables.

Regular employees were able to view and download their payslips as PDF files through the Employee Dashboard and to update their personal contact information. Access to these features was restricted to the Regular Employee role, as defined by the role-based access control configuration.
432 | Reporting Module Results
433 | The Reporting Module (M5) enabled the Finance Officer to generate a payroll summary report and a government contribution report for a selected month and year. The reports correctly aggregated the gross pay and the SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, and BIR withholding tax of all employees, and the column totals were verified to equal the sum of the corresponding individual payroll records. Both reports were successfully exported to PDF.
434 | User Management and Deduction Table Management Results
435 | Through the User Management Module (M2), the System Administrator was able to create, update, deactivate, and reactivate user accounts and assign user roles. Role-based access control prevented each user class from accessing functions outside its assigned role. Through the Deduction Table Management Module (M6), the Finance Officer was able to update the SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, and BIR contribution tables directly through the system interface; the updated rates were applied to the payroll computations immediately without requiring any modification to the source code.
436 | Testing Results
437 | The testing procedures described in the methodology were executed and produced the following results. Unit testing of the Payroll Calculation Engine confirmed that each formula — the SSS bracket lookup, PhilHealth rate application, Pag-IBIG cap enforcement, BIR TRAIN Law annualization, and Net Pay derivation — matched the manually computed expected values based on the official government tables. Integration testing confirmed the data flow from the MySQL database through the REST API to the calculation engine, from the calculation engine to payslip generation, and from the Reporting Module to the export functionality, and all REST API endpoints were verified . System testing confirmed the stability and correct behavior of the complete system in an operational environment. User Acceptance Testing was conducted with simulated end users representing the Finance Officer, Regular Employee, and System Administrator, and all defined acceptance criteria were met. Performance and security testing confirmed responsive payroll processing and report generation, and the protection of payroll data, employee records, and authentication tokens from unauthorized access. Regression testing was performed after every bug fix and confirmed that previously working features, particularly in the calculation engine, remained functional.
438 | Insights and Issues Encountered
439 | Several insights and issues were encountered during the development and testing of the system. First, the team initially computed the BIR withholding tax on the full gross monthly pay. During testing, it was identified that BIR Revenue Regulations No. 11-2018 requires the withholding tax to be computed on the monthly taxable income, which is the gross pay minus the employee’s mandatory SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG contributions. The calculation engine was corrected accordingly, and the correction was verified across all test cases. Second, government contribution rates change periodically; this finding motivated the development of the Deduction Table Management Module so that updated rates can be applied without modifying the source code. Third, because the application persists all records in a centralized MySQL database accessed through the server’s REST API, employees and the Finance Officer always see consistent, up-to-date data regardless of the device or browser they use. Data consistency is therefore maintained centrally on the server rather than on the client.

Second, a secure role-based web application provides distinct dashboards for the Finance Officer, Regular Employee, and System Administrator. Third, digital payslip generation and PDF download are accessible through the Employee Dashboard. Fourth, the Reporting Module generates payroll summary reports and government contribution reports and exports them in PDF format. Fifth, the Deduction Table Management Module allows the Finance Officer to update government contribution rates without modifying the system’s source code. Finally, the accuracy, usability, and functionality of the system were evaluated through unit, integration, system, performance, security, and regression testing, as well as user acceptance testing conducted with simulated end users for each user class.

445 | DISCUSSIONS, CONCLUSIONS, AND RECOMMENDATIONS
446 | This chapter presents the discussion of the results of the study, the conclusions drawn from the findings, and the recommendations for the future improvement and extension of the RCC-APS.
447 | Discussion
448 | The results presented in the preceding chapter demonstrate that the RCC-APS successfully fulfills the objectives of the study. The Payroll Calculation Engine produced deductions that matched the manual computations performed against the official government contribution tables, confirming the compliance of the system with the Social Security Act of 2018, the Universal Health Care Act, the HDMF Law, and the TRAIN Law. In particular, the correction of the BIR withholding tax computation from the full gross pay to the monthly taxable income — the gross pay minus the mandatory SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG contributions — aligned the system with the annualization rules under BIR Revenue Regulations No. 11-2018, and the consistent results across all test cases validated the reliability of the calculation engine. The role-based dashboards for the three user classes, the digital payslip generation with PDF download, the payroll summary and government contribution reports, and the configurable deduction tables all functioned as designed. The results of the unit, integration, system, performance, security, and regression tests, as well as the user acceptance testing conducted with simulated end users, confirmed the accuracy, usability, and functionality of the system. These findings are consistent with the review of related literature, which emphasized that automation reduces processing time and human error and that role-based access control improves security and accountability. The system likewise addresses the gaps identified among related capstone projects by implementing full compliance with the BIR TRAIN Law tax schedule, a dedicated deduction table management module, and differentiated access controls for three user classes.
449 | Conclusions
450 | Based on the findings of the study, the following conclusions are drawn. The RCC-APS successfully automates the computation of the Gross Pay, the mandatory government deductions (SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, and BIR withholding tax), and the Net Pay of each employee in compliance with the applicable Philippine labor and tax laws. The system provides a secure, role-based web application with distinct dashboards for the Finance Officer, Regular Employee, and System Administrator, effectively enforcing role-based access control across all features. The digital payslip generation and PDF download feature provides employees with transparent, self-service access to their compensation records. The Reporting Module enables the Finance Officer to generate payroll summary reports and government contribution reports and to export them in PDF format. The Deduction Table Management Module allows the Finance Officer to update government contribution rates without modifying the system’s source code. The centralized MySQL database and REST API endpoints keep the data consistent across all users of the system. The accuracy, usability, and functionality of the system were validated through unit, integration, system, performance, security, and regression testing, as well as through user acceptance testing conducted with simulated end users for each user class. Overall, the study concludes that the RCC-APS is a functional, accurate, and legally compliant payroll solution tailored to the operational context of Republic Central Colleges.
451 | Recommendations
452 | The following recommendations are offered for the future improvement and extension of the RCC-APS. First, the BIR withholding tax computation should be continuously monitored against amendments to the TRAIN Law and its implementing regulations, and the annualized taxable income method should be retained and periodically revalidated. Second, the integration with the partner HRIS should be extended to automate the import of attendance, overtime, and employee data, thereby reducing manual data entry. Third, the MySQL database schema could be expanded to support additional statutory deductions, loan amortizations, and other benefits, further strengthening concurrency, data consistency, and compliance coverage. Fourth, the system could incorporate additional statutory benefits and deductions — such as SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG loan amortizations, withholding tax on other compensation, and other government-mandated contributions — to further expand its compliance coverage. Fifth, automated testing should be expanded to cover the reporting and user management modules, and the test suite should be maintained alongside future system changes. Sixth, actual institutional deployment with the Finance Office of Republic Central Colleges is recommended to validate the system under real payroll conditions. Finally, future researchers may build upon this study by evaluating the system through formal usability studies and by exploring the integration of the RCC-APS with other institutional systems.

1576 | Project Description
1577 | The RCC-APS is a web-based payroll automation system developed for Republic Central Colleges. It computes employee Gross Pay, Net Pay, and government-mandated deductions (SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, and BIR withholding tax) for two employee classifications — Teaching Personnel (paid hourly) and Non-Teaching Personnel (paid a fixed monthly salary). The system integrates with a partner-developed HRIS for employee data and provides digital payslips, reporting, deduction and bonus table management, and a payroll analytics dashboard.
1578 | General Objective
1579 | To design, develop, and evaluate a web-based automated payroll system for Republic Central Colleges that accurately computes employee salaries, mandatory government deductions, and net pay in compliance with Philippine labor and tax laws.

2609 | Market research1. INTRODUCTION AND OBJECTIVES
2610 | This market research examines the existing payroll solutions used and developed by academic institutions in the Philippines. It compares open-source and documented systems built for colleges and universities with the RCC-APS Automated Payroll System of Republic Central Colleges (RCC). The purpose is to identify the strengths and limitations of existing solutions and to position RCC-APS as a tailored alternative for RCC.
2611 | The specific objectives of this research are to:
2612 | ·         identify the payroll solutions currently used or developed in academic institutions;
2613 | ·         compare their features against those of the RCC-APS system;
2614 | ·         determine the gaps left by existing solutions; and
2615 | ·         highlight the unique advantages of RCC-APS that answer the question, “What is unique about this system compared to other payroll systems?”
2616 | 2. MARKET OVERVIEW
2617 | In many educational institutions in the Philippines, payroll is still processed through manual records, paper forms, or spreadsheet software such as Microsoft Excel. Schools use a mix of full-time (non-teaching) staff and teaching personnel who are compensated by hourly load. As a result, payroll is repetitive, prone to human error, and time-consuming.
2618 | Several colleges and universities have responded by developing their own automated payroll systems. These systems vary in technology, security, and functionality. This research reviews three such systems and a manual baseline, comparing them with the RCC-APS system to show how RCC-APS addresses the specific needs of an academic institution.
2619 | 3. EXISTING SOLUTIONS IN THE MARKET
2620 | 3.1 Manual Process and Spreadsheets (Excel)
2621 | ·         No software cost and readily familiar to office staff.
2622 | ·         High risk of arithmetic errors, slow processing, difficulty tracking leaves and absences, and no reliable audit trail.
2623 | ·         Requires manual computation and re-entry of statutory rates for every employee every pay period.
2624 | 3.2 Payroll Management System for Quezon City University (QCU)
2625 | ·         A desktop application built with Java and JavaFX/Swing, connected to a relational database.
2626 | ·         Provides employee management, monthly payroll computation, time tracking, reporting, and role-based access for HR personnel and administrators.
2627 | ·         Limited to a desktop environment; no editable statutory tables and no analytics dashboard.
2628 | 3.3 Customized Payroll System for Bago City College (BCC)
2629 | ·         An automated payroll system developed for Bago City College using a custom process.
2630 | ·         Computes gross pay based on the Daily Time Record (DTR) and handles GSIS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, bank loans, cash advances, and tax deductions.
2631 | ·         Features limited to payroll computation and report generation; no editable statutory tables and no analytics dashboard.
2632 | 3.4 Employee Payroll System for Bestlink College of the Philippines
2633 | ·         A web-based payroll system that manages employee records, timesheets, taxes, wages, and basic pay.
2634 | ·         Computes hourly wages and leave days and generates printable payslips.
2635 | ·         Accessible by the admin or client only; no employee self-service and no analytics dashboard.

2708 | 6. UNIQUENESS OF RCC-APS: WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT
2709 | The following points summarize why the RCC-APS Automated Payroll System stands out from other payroll systems. 
2710 | ·         RCC-APS is purposely designed for Republic Central Colleges. It is not a generic product modified to fit a school; it is built around the exact payroll structure of RCC.
2711 | ·         The system natively supports two compensation models: Teaching personnel paid by hours worked, and Non-Teaching personnel paid a fixed monthly salary with absences deducted over 22 working days.
2712 | ·         RCC-APS connects to the RCC HRIS to automatically compute teaching hours and detect absences (no clock-in and no approved leave = absent), reducing manual data entry.
2713 | ·         SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, and BIR withholding tables are built in and editable, keeping the system compliant with Philippine law without manual rate lookup.
2714 | ·         Employees can view and download their digital payslips, and the finance officer can generate reports and analytics in minutes.

2832 | ·         Unlike the other systems, RCC-APS integrates directly with the RCC Human Resource Information System (HRIS) to automatically detect absences and compute teaching hours, reducing manual data entry and human error.
2833 | ·         While the other systems only generate reports, RCC-APS provides a full analytics dashboard showing payroll cost, government deductions, year-to-date totals, and monthly trends.
2834 | ·         The other systems are designed for their specific institutions (QCU, BCC, or Bestlink); RCC-APS is tailor-fit for Republic Central Colleges, reflecting its exact payroll structure and statutory requirements.
2835 | 4. WHY RCC-APS IS TAILOR-FIT FOR RCC
2836 | The RCC-APS Automated Payroll System was not copied from a generic product; it was built around the specific needs of Republic Central Colleges. The following points summarize why RCC-APS is uniquely suited to RCC and answer the question, “What makes this system different from other payroll systems?”
2837 | ·       Teaching (hourly) and Non-Teaching (monthly) personnel are handled differently, matching the actual compensation model of the College.
2838 | ·      All statutory deductions (SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, BIR, and the 13th month pay) are computed automatically and reflect the latest Philippine regulations.
2839 | ·    The system connects to the RCC HRIS to pull attendance data, automatically detecting absences and computing teaching hours without manual key-in.
2840 | ·  Employees can view and download their digital payslips, and the finance officer can generate comprehensive reports and analytics in minutes.
2841 | · RCC-APS uses MySQL, a stable and widely supported database, consistent with modern, deployment-ready systems used in higher education.
